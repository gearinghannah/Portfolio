PLACEMENT YEAR REPORT

This is a summary of my work that I did during my 9-month placement at the Institute NeuroMyoGène in Lyon, France. My final report was titled "Investigating the metastatic dissemination routes of medulloblastoma using a novel embryonic chick model."

Over the course of my placement, I gained icredible exposure to wet lab skills and advanced imaging methods, such as light sheet microscopy. I was able to immerse myself in the study of Group 3 medulloblastoma; the prevalence, the aetiology, the range of factors involved in the development, and what the current treatments are. In addition to the microscopy, I developed my wet lab skills with experience handling cell cultures, grafting cancer cells, dissecting samples, cryosectioning and learning the pros and cons of the chick embryo! Most importantly, the whole placement was amazing to meet the team, navigate the research environment internationally, and to take my first professional step into science.

An image of a chick embryo where there is a bright cluster of Group 3 medulloblastoma cells present in the rhombic lip area - the area where the cancer typically emerges before metastasising around the body. The cells have been transfected with GFP, giving it the green colour.

A schematic I developed to show the bidirectional nature of the cerebro-spinal fluid within the central canal of the embryo, giving us insight into how to consider grafting the cancer cells into this area of the chick, and how this might affect the metastatic route.

A series of slides where I mounted several coronal cryosections of the chick embryo head. This is then immunostained and visualised under the microscope to observe the cancer cells.

This is the scientific abstract, explaining the background context, my aims, methods and results.

This is the summary figure of my experiments grafting the medulloblastoma cells into the central canal of the chicks at day 1 of development. I collected them all at the same stage and visualised them to see the graft point (Dil), the metabolically active (green) and metabolically inactive (red) cancer cells. I quantified the migration of these cells between the hindlimb and forelimb, resulting in a significant decrease in migration in the metabolically active cancer cells, suggesting an active anchoring mechanism that takes place within the central canal, potentially preceding the invasion process before metastasis.

This is further analysis of the active anchoring mechanism where additional samples were cut sagitally to get a better view of the central canal and the medulloblastoma cells within it. (a) and (b) show some intercellular connections that might suggest vesicular elements contributing to this mechanism - more work needs to be done to verify this. (c) shows cross-sections of the central canal with the bright green cancel cells embedded within the surrounding tissue, further suggesting an anchoring process happening.
