SCIENCE IN CULTURE

DRUGS & THE BRAIN GROUP PRESENTATION

This was a group project for a Drugs & the Brain university module where we watched 'First Do No Harm', directed by Jim Abrahams, where we can see a family struggling to treat their son's epilepsy, alongside the societal attitudes and approaches to the treatment at the time. We discussed the aetiology of epilepsy and the pharmacological agents that can be used to treat it.

The Concept

The presentation brief was to describe the causes and effect of epilepsy, what the symptoms are and what treatment options are available currently. Most importantly, the task was to also highlight the societal impact of living with epilepsy, particularly when treatment options are ineffective, or when the origin of the epilepsy is unknown.

The Process

We shared the workload, researching the context of the film, what pharmacological agents were employed as the first-line treatments, and how that has changed with recent preclinical and clinical studies regarding anticonvulsants.

What struck me most about the film was the portrayal of the family experience and the underlying emotional and financial costs that can sometimes be overlooked when clinicians and scientists are developing novel therapies for patients. Witnessing the sacrifice of the parents and the boy's siblings really put into perspective the importance of keeping the patients at the heart of the work, and making sure that they are being listened to.

Skills Developed

Neuropharmacology

Bioethics

Patient Safety

Visual Storytelling

Final Outcome

The project was given a grade of 85%, taking account of scientific accuracy and visual presentation. I was very happy to engage with neuroscience within cultural media which was quite rare throughout my degree. Ultimately, the module taught me a lot about neuropharmacology and highlighted that we need to remember the humanity that drives the research, not just the basis of the mechanisms at a microscopic level.
