PUBLIC HEALTH

PM SOCIETY FUTURE COMMUNICATORS COMPETITION

I designed and developed a storyboard for a 1-2 minute YouTube video, leveraging AI tools to generate visual materials and showcase the central campaign messaging; identifying and being awarene of everyday behaviours that might be putting us at risk of harm.

The Concept

Before coming up with campaign ideas, I thought of memorable public health campaigns that used certain aspects very well to capture the audience's attention and make the messaging stand out, whilst also empowering people to change their attitude. I thought of 'Dumb Ways to Die' which went viral for their fun illustrations paired with a catchy theme song, and even their interactive mobile game. I also was reminded of generational campaigns, like 'Every Little Helps' where the slogan elegantly delivers a core message, but is simple enough to be applied to a spectrum of scenarios.

I decided that I wanted to use a strategy which combined visual cues coupled with the slogan 'Think Twice'. The visual cues did the heavy lifting, illustrating everyday scenes that were instantly recognisable, allowing viewers to quickly adopt a more personal and invested perspective, and building a greater sense of self-responsibility when considering their behaviours that might be contributing to the rise of antimicrobial resistance (AMR).

The Process

This is an example of one of the everyday scenarios that I conceptualised which linked a lack of awareness to danger and future risks. The visual of the dog slowly approaching the oncoming traffic, unknowingly to their owner, symbolises how it is easy to miss a developing risk when we're not paying attention. It also demonstrates that it's not done intentionally, yet can still bear consequence.

The storyboard describes the audio cues in more detail - how the volume and pacing of the ambient sounds can be employed as a tool to build tension and indicate moments of increased danger.

The final slide of the deck demonstrates how the video would be measured according to the primary KPIs (Key Performance Indicators) which are Reach, Understanding and Attitude Change. The data points shown are examples of how we might quantify certain aspects of the video, and how they could be compared to show the number of people the video has reached, and if there was both short- and long-term attitude change towards AMR awareness and behaviour that may contribute to it.

Final Outcome

I submitted the storyboard as part of the Future Communicators Competition 2026 and am eager to hear any feedback. I was very happy with the outcome of this project and was very happy to gain more experience with visual storytelling, as well as gaining a better understanding of how to conceptualise a public health campaign. I love taking part in work that asks me to engage with science and requires me to combine it with creativity and marketing.
