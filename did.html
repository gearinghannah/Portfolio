ESSAY

DISSOCIATIVE IDENTITY DISORDER

This essay was part of my Science Communication module where we were given 3 topics within hot neuroscience research areas to choose from. I decided to look into the current understanding of Dissociative Identity Disorder (DID) and the new discoveries being made about the pathology of the disease as more advanced imaging technology meant a more comprehensive look at underlying structural changes in the brain.

Not only was the essay meant to explain the pathology of the disease, but I had to show how the new research contrasted the previous hypothesis to how DID emerged in people. In addition to adapting the understanding of the condition, I had to illustrate how this changed the way clinicians might treat and diagnose DID individuals.

The advanced imaging techniques were discussed as a way to improve current diagnostic strategies. Furthermore, I looked deeper into the hormonal aspects of the condition that seemed to be underrepresented within the literature when discussing the treatment options - an aspect I thought to be very pertinent as DID is more often coupled with traumatic events in childhood as well as PTSD - things that are known to shrink brain size and alter morphology. Finally, I also explained the pharmacological options that were available to treat the comorbidities, like depression, that are usually observed in DID patients.

This was the intro for the essay, clearly explaining the background for the topic and the prevalence of DID in society.

Final Outcome

I achieved 92% for this essay which I was very happy with. This essay was one of the first assignments where we were encouraged to communicate science outside of a lab report, without statistical analysis or explicit practical knowledge. It made me realise that this was something I would like to keep doing and I loved researching this topic as it is a great example of how modern techniques can evolve our understanding of an under-studied condition, and how a holistic perspective might be used to view the interconnectivity of neuroscience with the endocrine system.
